# Ransom

An iOS screen-time blocker. You pick the apps; Ransom shields them. Push-ups and
squats, counted by the camera, earn minutes into a bank; opening a blocked app
spends from it. SwiftUI, iOS 17+, on Apple's Screen Time frameworks.

**Tagline:** *Move a little. Scroll a little.*

---

## Context for whoever picks this up

Every line of this app was written on a Linux container with no Swift toolchain.
It was never compiled during authoring. For most of its life a GitHub Actions
macOS runner (`.github/workflows/build.yml`) was the only thing that had ever
type-checked it.

That is no longer the constraint: the current machine has Xcode 26.6 and Swift
6.3, so the app builds, installs and launches locally, and the loop is now a
minute rather than a CI round trip. Build against a real simulator before
believing anything.

That history still matters, though: **the code compiles and runs, but almost none
of it has been exercised.** No test has ever run. Treat runtime behaviour as
unverified.

## Build and run

```
open Ransom/Ransom.xcodeproj      # ⌘R to a simulator
```

`TESTING.md` covers the Apple-account realities in full. The short version:
Family Controls and App Groups are paid-account capabilities, the bundle IDs are
`com.ransom.*` and won't sign against another team, and §4 has a strip-down that
runs the whole intake flow in a simulator on a free Apple ID.

CI builds with `CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO` against a generic simulator, so a green
build says nothing about whether entitlements will provision.

## Layout

```
Ransom/
├── Ransom/                 App target
│   ├── Core/               Theme, models, AppModel store, StoreKit, notifications
│   ├── Onboarding/         16-step intake flow
│   ├── Workout/            Rep detection (Vision + CoreMotion) and the set screen
│   ├── Blocking/           Screen Time authorization and shield control
│   ├── Mascot/             Rex - eight illustrated poses, cross-faded
│   ├── Home/ Stats/ Settings/ Paywall/ Components/
├── RansomShared/           Compiled into all four targets
├── RansomShield/           Shield UI extension - the block screen
├── RansomShieldAction/     Shield button handling
├── RansomMonitor/          DeviceActivityMonitor - puts the shield back
└── Config/                 Info.plists, entitlements, StoreKit config
```

Four targets share `RansomShared/` via folder-synced groups. The app and its
extensions are separate processes that coordinate **only** through the App Group
(`group.com.ransom.app`) and Darwin notifications. `UnlockLedger` is that shared
bus; `RansomCore.Key` holds every key it uses. **If you add state the shield
needs, it goes in `UnlockLedger` and its key goes in `RansomCore.Key`** — a
missing key there was the first bug CI ever caught.

## Product decisions worth not re-litigating

**The economy is a minute bank, and the tariff is gone.** Sets and (when enabled)
steps earn minutes into `UnlockLedger.bankedMinutes`; opening a blocked app spends
from that balance. One flat rate per set: the escalating per-unlock tariff was
retired because with a bank it would mean the exchange rate moving under the user
mid-set. `Tariff.swift` still exists but nothing references it - delete it when
you are sure the escalation is not coming back.

Three rules the bank has already been broken by once each, so do not undo them:
 * **Earning never starts the clock.** `completeSet` and `UnlockLedger.spend`
   only move the balance; `ScreenTimeManager.grantEarnedTime` is the single place
   that grants time. Two of those granting at once made a 15-minute spend open the
   apps for 30, twice, in two different functions.
 * **Earned and spent are different numbers.** `todayMinutesEarned` comes from
   history, `todayMinutesUnlocked` from `ledger.spentMinutesToday`. They were the
   same value before the bank and conflating them reports a long walk as screen
   time used.
 * **The bank clears at midnight** (`bankedMinutes` checks `bankDay` on read). A
   balance that carries forever becomes a stockpile and the habit stops being daily.

**The daily goal is screen time, not reps.** `profile.goalDailyMinutes` is set by
the user on the "What's the target?" step and drives `AppModel.todayAllowance`.
A rep-based daily goal sells the wrong thing: a big rep number means a lot of
unlocks were bought, so hitting it means they scrolled all day. It has been
removed from Home and the plan screen; `RansomPlan.dailyRepGoal` still exists and
should probably go.

**Difficulty is committed to for a run.** Tier and length are chosen together
(intake "Pick your pace", and Settings), applied instantly. Upgrading is always
allowed and restarts the clock at the new tier; dropping to an easier tier is
refused while `profile.isCommitted`, as is shortening a live run. On expiry the
tier stays and the lock simply lifts. Settings requires a second confirmation
naming the real date. The whole mechanism fails if it can be softened from inside
a craving, which is the entire point.

**Every projection reads one curve.** `RansomPlan.reduction(onDay:)` models the
habit shrinking 35% over four weeks. Reps paid, hours saved and the plan
screen's chart all derive from it. An earlier version held unlocks flat forever
and quoted *108,000 push-ups a year* directly beneath a chart promising screen
time falls — both figures computed, mutually contradictory. Don't reintroduce a
projection that ignores the curve.

**Numbers are computed, never written.** Three reviewers independently guessed
20,000 / 15,000 / 17,000 for a figure whose real range was 12,775–98,550. Any
quantity shown to the user derives from their answers.

**Pricing:** $4.99/week or $49.99/year, annual preselected. Two products in one
subscription group, both with a 3-day trial. `Config/Ransom.storekit` lets the
paywall run locally with no App Store Connect setup.

**The intake opens with a three-tap cold open** (`ColdOpenStep`): looping Seedance
clips of Rex with his phone, copy typed on per glyph, ending by turning the story
on the reader. Copy is deliberately written for an 11-year-old reader; beat 2's
short-arms joke is the product thesis in disguise and must survive any rewrite.

**Only push-ups and squats are offered.** `Exercise.selectable` withholds steps:
the pedometer counts them whether or not anyone is trying, so an ordinary day's
walking would fund an evening's scrolling. All the step code, pricing and the
`StepTracker` still work; restoring it is one line, but it needs a daily cap or a
baseline subtraction first.

**One set costs the same for everyone on a tier.** A self-reported fitness level
used to scale the rep target, so "Standard" meant 10 push-ups for one person and
14 for another, which made the number unexplainable and was a guess made on day
one about something that changes as you get stronger. Moving up a tier is how
that is handled now. `FitnessLevel` and its intake step were removed entirely.

**The intake is 17 steps** and the order is deliberate: the user names their own
apps and hours *before* the reality check, so the number is theirs, not ours.
The identity step ("I'm someone who gets stronger without going to a gym") is
echoed back on the plan screen and the paywall. The first-rep step asks for five
reps before anything is paid for, and is skippable without scolding.

**Rex is a tangerine T-rex** (`#F06027`), not the green gecko earlier commits
describe. Eight static poses in `Assets.xcassets`, cross-faded — the push-up
pose blends top and bottom frames on `depth`. Two more (`RexWave`, `RexThumbsUp`)
are intake-only and drawn by `Onboarding/RexIntakeArt.swift` rather than added
to `RexPose`; they sit on the same 451×640 canvas, feet on the same baseline.

**Rex's voice is a supportive friend, not a bouncer.** The funnel never says
pay, price, cost, charge or tariff to the user. Reps *unlock* minutes; a heavy
day *asks a little more per set*; the late-night rate is explained as leaning on
the habit they said they wanted gone. The escalation is still shown in full
(the ladder card on the pace step), it's just not framed as a penalty.

## The push-up counter, and why it is set the way it is

`Workout/PoseRepCounter.swift`. Tuned against two screen recordings of real sets
with known counts, not from theory. Every constant below was moved because a
frame-by-frame reading of that footage said so, and reasoning from first
principles got several of them backwards first:

 * **Head-on, not side-on.** Filmed from the side a plank lies flat across the
   frame, which is the pose Vision detects worst. Head-on it reads as an upright
   body. This also means the upper arm is foreshortened, so a genuinely deep rep
   bottoms out around **116-129 degrees, not 90**. Tune against that, not anatomy.
 * **Elbows are mandatory, shoulders corroborate.** Elbow angle is the only signal
   that means "push-up". Shoulder drop is reliable *during* reps (0.5-0.8 shoulder
   widths) and useless *between* them (rests produced 0.9-2.0), so it can never
   lead. When the shoulders are tracked through 70%+ of a rep and drop 0.35+, the
   elbow gate relaxes to 20 degrees of travel, which is what rescues close grip.
 * **Close grip is the hard case.** Tucked elbows read 25-31 degrees of travel on
   real reps against a 35 gate, and the wrists vanish behind the head at the
   bottom (hence `jointHold = 0.7s`). Wide grip has never been a problem.
 * **Knee push-ups are caught by knee *height*, never knee *angle*.** Head-on the
   leg points at the lens so hip, knee and ankle project to nearly one point and
   the angle is noise - the first version rejected perfect form and passed the
   cheat. Ankle lift read -0.02 planking and +0.15 kneeling; knee-vs-wrist height
   separates 0.30-0.44 from -0.26-0.13. Leg samples are taken only from frames
   where the elbows were bent, or resting on your knees between reps taints the
   next one.
 * **No legs visible means counted, not doubted.** Punishing a badly framed camera
   as though it were cheating is the worse error.
 * **Arming is a stillness test**, not a timer: a hand reaching in to prop the
   phone gives a straight-arm reading and satisfies any time-based guard. This is
   what stops the phantom rep that appears before the user has lain down.
 * **A rejection must be visible.** Every frame used to overwrite `formHint`, so
   reasons were wiped ~30ms after being written and the counter looked broken
   because the only thing that explained it was invisible. Hints now hold 2.5s,
   take the headline, and turn the camera border red; green means counting.

`-RansomDebugHUD 1` puts the live readout back under the camera. Reading those
numbers off a screen recording is how all of the above was established; if the
counter regresses, ask for a screen recording with known counts and read it frame
by frame rather than guessing.

## Open, in rough priority order

- **Blocking has still never been tested.** Family Controls needs a paid Apple
  Developer account; every device build so far strips the entitlements, so the
  shield, the monitor extension and the whole spend-to-unlock path are unverified.
  This is the largest unknown in the app by a wide margin.
- **The paywall still quotes the retired economy**: "10 push-ups unlocks 15
  minutes" should say banks, and "4,400 push-ups along the way" is computed from
  the old per-unlock model. Under the bank the honest figure is far larger and far
  less flattering, so it is a product decision, not a copy fix.
- `RansomPlan.projectedReps` and `expectedUnlocksPerDay` still model unlocks.
- **No app icon yet.** Four concepts generated (Rex silhouette, Rex head, padlock,
  spiked R); the two vector ones are real SVGs. None chosen or installed.
- **Swift 6 is a project, not a landmine.** The default build is warning-free, and
  the seven sites that blocked `SWIFT_VERSION=6` outright are fixed. Building with
  `SWIFT_STRICT_CONCURRENCY=complete` still reports **47 issues across 12 files**,
  concentrated in `Core/Haptics.swift` (14), `Workout/RepEngine.swift` (9) and
  `Core/SubscriptionManager.swift` (8). That is the real remaining scope; flipping
  the language mode before working through it will not build.
- `Tariff.swift` is dead code. `RansomPlan.dailyRepGoal` nearly so.
- `Onboarding/RexIntakeArt.swift` duplicates the canvas maths in `Mascot/RexPose`,
  an artefact of two agents owning different directories. Worth folding together.
- `UserProfile.gender` and `GenderStep` are dead.
- Steps are built and priced but withheld from `Exercise.selectable`.
- Promotional offers need server-side signing that does not exist.
- No tests of any kind.
- Trademark clearance and an App Store name check for "Ransom".

## Conventions

- Comments explain *why*, not *what*, and earn their place. Several in this
  codebase record a bug that has already been made once — leave those.
- Debug-only hooks are wrapped in `#if DEBUG`. `-RansomStartStep <step>` opens
  the app on any intake screen with a seeded profile; that's how CI screenshots
  every screen without a UI-test target. `-RansomSeedName ""` blanks the seeded
  first name so the no-name states can be captured. `-RansomPro 1` skips intake and the
  paywall, which is the only way to reach the set screen on a real device.
  `-RansomBank <n>` seeds banked minutes so the spend UI is reachable,
  `-RansomDebugHUD 1` shows the rep-counter readout, `-RansomTab <0-2>` picks a
  tab and `-RansomSeedName ""` captures the unnamed states.
- Everything stays on device. No account, no server, no analytics. Pose
  detection runs through Vision locally and no video is recorded or uploaded.
